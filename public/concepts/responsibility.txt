#RESPONSIBILITY

BLURB
Follow through on commitments; be proactive and adaptable.

PITCH
Set honest goals, follow up honestly, and communicate honestly when unable to follow through.

PARAGRAPH DESCRIPTION
When working with others, one should take responsibility for one’s own contribution and communicate proactively. This means that one should contribute according to one’s abilities and ethical principles, keeping in mind not to set unrealistic expectations. After carefully setting expectations, it is crucial to think about accountability structures. To act responsibly, it is vital to recognize what is and is not within your locus of control. One can improve one’s self-control and grit to an extent, which will help set and achieve ambitious goals, but it is important to recognize the limits of individual effort too. When setbacks arise, it is crucial to acknowledge your responsibility for areas where you have fallen short and to be proactive about finding solutions. Others should be told about problems with enough advance notice for them to adjust their plans and deal with the resulting challenges.

GENERAL EXAMPLE
You have a final project team that has never worked together, and decide to split tasks and assign members responsibilities within the group. You decide to plan a meeting every other week to check-in and ensure members keep up with their responsibilities. To hold yourself accountable, you also schedule at least 45 minutes every day after class to work on your final project, during which you arrange to have your friend take your phone away from you to enhance focus. These strategies enhance your efficiency, and you stay on track. After several meetings, your team realizes one of your coworkers is not showing up. Several of you approach him to ask if he is having difficulties, but he is hard to reach. By the end of the semester, even though all other members kept each other accountable and finished their tasks, this member hadn't completed his part. Whether he simply lacked self-control or was limited in his contribution by external factors, his contribution hurt the overall team grade. Next time, you will consider how to embed accountability and adaptability in pursuit of your goal more effectively. While the individual’s actions and circumstances are his own, the team could have worked together to identify those factors that are within their control, such as proactively mitigating the impact of his poor contribution and setting a point at which they would seek external advice regarding the composition of their team.

Footnote: The application not only explains how particularities of responsibility for different members of the group combined to lead to a specific outcome in the social system but also details some of the strategies (weekly meetings, no phone) used to target specific facets of responsibility and why they were effective. It demonstrates a separation between what is and what is not within the team’s locus of control. It also highlights what could be done differently in the future by taking ownership of the teamwork dynamic and intervening when external factors (in this case, a teammate) create challenges.

SELF STUDY QUESTIONS
1. What qualities and strategies can you develop to achieve more ambitious goals (as an individual or team)?

   Answer: Strategies include:
   
   - cultivating an internal over an external locus of control, 
   - planning and the planning fallacy, 
   - self-control, 
   - shaping and incentivizing behavior that is aligned with acting responsibly (related to #shapingbehavior), 
   - using superordinate goal-setting or showing the tenacity to work toward a goal over the long term (grit), 
   - accountability strategies, 
   - adaptability strategies

2. What is the danger of the above strategies of #responsibility as focusing on improving output?

   Answer: Although grit, internal locus of control, and self-control can work to an extent to become a more responsible adult and achieve the goals you want to achieve, one should not get the idea that these can help you overcome any obstacle. Many people face external constraints to their ambitions. Developing an honest locus of control is likely more healthy. Sometimes, acknowledging the limits of your control is actually taking control of a difficult situation.

3. What is a locus of control?

   Answer: A locus of control refers to an individual's belief about the extent to which they have control over their own life and the events that happen to them. It can be internal or external. An internal locus of control means that an individual believes they have control over their own life and the events that happen to them. In contrast, an external locus of control means that an individual believes that external factors such as luck, fate, or other people have more control over their life and the events that happen to them. Go to the Key Resources section for a great resource!

4. How can someone use their locus of control?

   Answer: There are several ways, including:
   
   - Taking responsibility for actions and decisions you made yourself. 
   -  Focus on what you can control and let go of what you cannot.
   -  Setting realistic goals in the first place and working towards them.
   -  Developing a positive mindset and reframing negative thoughts. 
   - Building resilience and coping skills to respond to challenges and setbacks.
   - Seeking support from others when needed.

5. What are some benefits of an internal locus of control?

   Answer: Having an internal locus of control has been linked to various positive outcomes, including higher levels of motivation, better coping skills, and increased success in achieving personal and professional goals. Individuals who believe they have more control over their life and what happens to them are also more likely to take proactive steps to improve their lives and are less likely to feel helpless or hopeless in the face of adversity.But! Be careful because many things are indeed outside of your control. Believing everything is can lead to disillusionment or being overly self-critical. Increasing pressure on yourself can cause stress/burnout and a refusal to delegate. Having a balanced (and accurate!) locus of control is always best. Especially within larger systems, there are people whose internal locus of control is limited by external factors. Criticizing oneself in such circumstances misses the big picture that we also want students at Minerva to think about!

6. What are some strategies for overcoming procrastination and improving follow-through?

   Answer: One strategy for overcoming procrastination is to break down goals into smaller, more manageable tasks that can be completed in shorter periods. This helps create a sense of progress and momentum, which is motivating. Another strategy is to create a specific action plan with deadlines and to hold oneself accountable by sharing this plan with others. Additionally, it can be helpful to identify and address any underlying emotional or psychological barriers that may be contributing to procrastination, such as fear of failure or perfectionism.

COMMON PITFALLS
- The application only focused on the colloquial use of responsibility, tagging an action as an application of #responsibility because the action showed the characteristics of acting responsibly without explaining specific strategies.
- The application did not go in-depth to describe how the individual showed thinking ahead of time in developing strategies, mindsets, or habits that would enable them to accomplish a stated goal.
- The application focuses only on establishing responsibility (morally, legally, etc.) without thinking about how to follow through effectively.
- Focusing on individual responsibility and an internal locus of control without acknowledging the constraining factors or responding to those realistically.

APPLYING THIS HC OUTSIDE MINERVA
- In personal relationships, responsibility might be relevant. We often have commitments toward our family members, partners, or friends. In these, we communicate honestly about expectations, show up for each other in critical moments, and might commit to the longevity of a relationship (through marriage, for example).
- In business, monthly team projects will require the team to schedule, coordinate, and ensure everyone is on the right track.
- When developing a business, you might go through periods of self-doubt. This is an instance where recognizing how #confidence might fluctuate and compel you to develop contingency plans, like “If I find myself doubting my capacities to build a business, I will open a journal I will make to archive other things I have built.”
- When doing creative work like producing a film or writing a novel, you might have to set up incentive structures to accomplish the task as well as reminders about why you want to do the work to help you during days when your motivation is lacking.
